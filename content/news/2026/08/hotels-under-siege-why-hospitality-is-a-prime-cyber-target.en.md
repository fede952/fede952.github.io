From ID theft at Italian check-ins to Milan-Cortina attacks and Booking.com breach, hotels face structural cyber risks that current rules only partially address.

{{< cyber-report severity="Medium" source="Cybersecurity360" target="Hotel industry" >}}

The hospitality sector is increasingly targeted by cybercriminals, as highlighted by three recent incidents: theft of identity documents during check-ins in Italy, campaigns against hotels in the Milan-Cortina area, and a breach involving Booking.com. These cases underscore a common structural vulnerability that persists across the industry.

{{< ad-banner >}}

Despite existing regulations, the hotel industry remains exposed due to its reliance on third-party platforms, handling of sensitive personal data, and often insufficient cybersecurity measures. The attacks demonstrate that current rules only partially mitigate the risks, leaving gaps that attackers exploit.

The pattern suggests that hotels need to adopt more robust security practices, including better protection of guest data, enhanced monitoring of third-party services, and compliance with evolving cybersecurity standards to reduce their attractiveness as targets.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

For SOC analysts, treat hotel booking and check-in systems as high-value targets for credential theft and data exfiltration. DevSecOps should prioritize hardening integrations with third-party platforms like Booking.com and enforce strict access controls on PII. Proactive threat hunting for anomalous access to guest records is essential, given the sector's attractiveness to attackers.
