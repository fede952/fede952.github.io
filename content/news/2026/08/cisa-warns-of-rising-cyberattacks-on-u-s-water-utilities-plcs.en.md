CISA alerts to increased attacks on internet-exposed PLCs in water and wastewater systems, urging utilities to harden defenses.

{{< cyber-report severity="High" source="BleepingComputer" target="Internet-exposed PLCs in water utilities" >}}

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has issued a warning about a significant uptick in cyberattacks targeting programmable logic controllers (PLCs) that are exposed to the internet within the water and wastewater sector. These attacks could disrupt critical operations, posing risks to public health and safety.

{{< ad-banner >}}

CISA's advisory emphasizes the need for water utilities to identify and secure any internet-facing PLCs, as these devices are often legacy systems with weak authentication and limited monitoring capabilities. The agency recommends implementing network segmentation, enforcing multi-factor authentication, and applying vendor patches promptly.

While the advisory does not specify particular threat actors or provide technical indicators of compromise, it underscores the growing threat landscape for critical infrastructure. Utilities are urged to review their cybersecurity posture and collaborate with CISA and sector partners to mitigate potential disruptions.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

For SOC analysts, this advisory is a reminder to inventory and monitor any ICS/SCADA devices that might be inadvertently exposed. DevSecOps engineers should prioritize network segmentation and ensure that PLCs are not directly reachable from the internet, as these systems often lack modern security controls. Treat this as a prompt to validate your incident response plans for OT environments, where traditional IT defenses may not apply.
