Google ramps up Chrome patching to twice a week after AI-assisted discovery finds more vulnerabilities in June than the previous 23 updates combined.

{{< cyber-report severity="Medium" source="Wired Security" target="Google Chrome browser" >}}

Google has announced a significant acceleration in Chrome's patching schedule, moving to twice-weekly updates. This change follows a surge in vulnerability discoveries attributed to AI-assisted bug hunting tools. In June alone, Chrome patched more security flaws than in the entire preceding set of 23 updates, indicating a dramatic increase in the rate of vulnerability identification.

{{< ad-banner >}}

The shift underscores the dual-edged nature of AI in cybersecurity: while it empowers defenders to find and fix bugs faster, it also implies that attackers may be leveraging similar techniques to exploit weaknesses more rapidly. For organizations relying on Chrome, this means a shorter window between patch releases and a greater urgency to deploy updates promptly to mitigate risk.

While no specific CVEs or CVSS scores were disclosed in the report, the sheer volume of patches suggests that some of these vulnerabilities could be severe. The new cadence reflects Google's proactive stance in staying ahead of threats, but it also places a heavier operational burden on IT and security teams to keep browsers current.
