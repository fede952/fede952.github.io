CERT-AGID warns of a fraudulent site impersonating ARERA, using the water social bonus as bait to harvest personal and financial data via typosquatting.

{{< cyber-report severity="Medium" source="CERT-AgID" target="Italian citizens and ARERA users" >}}

CERT-AGID has identified a fraudulent website that imitates the name and logo of ARERA, the Italian Regulatory Authority for Energy, Networks and Environment. The site lures victims with the promise of a refund related to the 'water social bonus', a legitimate measure designed to reduce water supply costs for households in economic or physical hardship.

{{< ad-banner >}}

The phishing campaign employs typosquatting techniques to increase the credibility of the fake domain, making it appear almost identical to the legitimate ARERA website. The goal is to trick users into disclosing personal and financial information, which can then be used for identity theft or financial fraud.

This incident highlights the ongoing threat of phishing campaigns that exploit well-known government or regulatory bodies. Users are advised to verify the authenticity of any communication claiming to offer refunds or bonuses, and to avoid clicking on links in unsolicited emails or messages.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

For SOC analysts, this campaign underscores the need to monitor for typosquatted domains related to critical public services. Implementing DNS filtering and educating users on verifying official channels can mitigate such threats. DevSecOps teams should consider integrating threat intelligence feeds that track lookalike domains to proactively block access.
