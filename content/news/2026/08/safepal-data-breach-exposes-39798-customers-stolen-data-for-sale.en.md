Cryptocurrency hardware wallet provider SafePal warns of a data breach affecting 39,798 customers after a flaw was exploited to steal order information; stolen data is now for sale.

{{< cyber-report severity="High" source="BleepingComputer" target="SafePal cryptocurrency hardware wallet customers" >}}

SafePal, a cryptocurrency hardware wallet provider, has disclosed a data breach that impacts approximately 39,798 customers. The breach occurred after a vulnerability was exploited to steal customer order information, and a threat actor is now claiming to be selling the stolen data.

{{< ad-banner >}}

The exact nature of the exploited flaw has not been disclosed, and no CVE identifier has been provided. The stolen data appears to be limited to order information, which may include names, addresses, and purchase details, but not necessarily wallet credentials or private keys.

This incident highlights the ongoing risk to cryptocurrency-related services, where even non-financial data can be valuable to attackers for phishing and social engineering campaigns. SafePal customers should be vigilant for targeted scams and monitor their accounts for suspicious activity.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

For SOC analysts, this breach underscores the importance of monitoring for data exfiltration from e-commerce and order management systems, not just core financial infrastructure. DevSecOps teams should ensure that any web-facing application, even those not directly handling funds, is patched promptly and has proper access controls. Additionally, since stolen order data can fuel highly convincing phishing attacks, organizations should implement email filtering and user awareness training focused on cryptocurrency-related threats.
