Microsoft attributes global hotel Wi-Fi attacks to Russia's Midnight Blizzard (APT29), using custom malware to breach Microsoft 365 accounts.

{{< cyber-report severity="High" source="BleepingComputer" target="Hospitality Wi-Fi networks and Microsoft 365 accounts" >}}

Microsoft has identified a global cyber campaign targeting hospitality Wi-Fi networks, attributing it to the Russian threat actor Midnight Blizzard, also known as APT29. The attacks leverage custom malware to compromise Microsoft 365 accounts, indicating a sophisticated and persistent threat.

{{< ad-banner >}}

The campaign underscores the risks associated with public Wi-Fi networks in hotels, which are often trusted by business travelers and can serve as a gateway to corporate credentials. APT29's use of custom malware suggests a tailored approach to evade detection and maintain access.

Organizations with employees who travel frequently should be aware of this threat and reinforce security measures, such as enforcing multi-factor authentication and monitoring for unusual account activity, especially when accessed from hotel networks.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

For SOC analysts, this campaign highlights the need to monitor authentication logs for anomalies originating from hospitality IP ranges. DevSecOps teams should ensure that VPN and MFA are mandatory for remote access, and consider network segmentation to limit the blast radius if a hotel Wi-Fi compromise occurs.
