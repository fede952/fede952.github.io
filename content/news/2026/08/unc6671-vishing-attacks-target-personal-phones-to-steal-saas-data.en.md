UNC6671 uses vishing to target financial services, private equity, and professional services, posing as IT help desk to steal SaaS data.

{{< cyber-report severity="High" source="The Hacker News" target="Financial services, private equity, professional services" >}}

A recent wave of cyber attacks has been attributed to the data extortion group UNC6671, which is targeting financial services, private equity, and professional services organizations. The group employs voice phishing (vishing) to compromise enterprise employees, posing as IT help desk staff to initiate mandatory, urgent security migrations.

{{< ad-banner >}}

Notably, UNC6671 often contacts employees via their personal phones, a tactic that bypasses corporate security monitoring and increases the likelihood of success. The ultimate goal is to steal sensitive data from Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) applications, likely for extortion purposes.

Organizations in the targeted sectors should be aware of this evolving threat and implement robust verification procedures for any unsolicited IT-related requests, especially those received on personal devices.

{{< netrunner-insight >}}

SOC analysts should educate employees about vishing tactics, emphasizing that IT staff will never ask for credentials or multifactor authentication codes over the phone. Implement strict policies for verifying any urgent security requests, and consider deploying vishing simulation exercises to test employee awareness. Additionally, monitor for unusual access patterns to SaaS applications, especially from personal devices or after hours.
